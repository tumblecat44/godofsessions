# Launch positioning

## The product in one sentence

**God of Sessions is the local control plane that decides what needs you now
and what can move safely overnight.**

It sits above the coding agents a developer already uses. It does not replace
their terminals, subscriptions, session formats, or native receipts.

## The one reason to install it

**One bedtime approval. Verified work by morning.**

The memorable outcome is not “all agents in one window.” It is ending the
night with one bounded decision and starting the morning with provider-owned
receipts plus observed workspace evidence.

## Primary user

A Mac developer who regularly has three or more active coding-agent tools or
sessions across Codex, Claude Code, Grok Build, Cursor, Hermes, or OpenClaw.
They already know how to start agents. Their bottleneck is deciding which work
deserves attention or scarce subscription capacity, and supervising safe work
after they walk away.

## Killer feature

**The bedtime briefing:** Morrow finds the one item that needs the operator,
ranks the work that can safely move tonight, freezes an exact time/route/
permission plan, and hands the operator one approval boundary. Morning Review
then joins the approved plan to provider receipts and bounded workspace
observations.

This is one feature with a before-and-after outcome, not a bundle of dashboard
features.

## Category and competitive boundary

Lori, agmux, and Dorchestrator make a strong promise around launching or
running many agents inside one workspace. God of Sessions should not compete
on that sentence.

| Multi-agent workspace | God of Sessions |
| --- | --- |
| Starts and switches agent sessions | Reads existing sessions across tools |
| Makes many terminals easier to operate | Reduces the operator decisions those terminals create |
| Centers the active workspace | Centers attention, capacity, approval, and morning evidence |
| “Run every agent here” | “Know what needs you, then approve one safe night” |

The durable category description is **local AI session control plane**.
“Night-shift operator” is the product story, not the technical category.

## Brand relationship

- **God of Sessions** is the product and repository name.
- **Morrow** is the recognizable character and the night-shift operator inside
  the product.
- Morrow can speak, inspect read-only control-plane tools, explain a ranked
  recommendation, and hand work to the approval surface.
- Morrow is not a model provider, a replacement coding agent, or an invisible
  autonomous executor.

For launch, the product name should lead and Morrow should make the behavior
memorable. Do not rename the product to Morrow.

## Launch copy hierarchy

1. **Stop being the queue.**
2. **Every session. One clear next move.**
3. **One bedtime approval. Verified work by morning.**
4. Morrow finds what needs you now—and what can safely move overnight.

The first line turns the operator bottleneck into a phrase people can repeat.
The second is the durable product catchphrase. The third is the concrete
install outcome, and the fourth explains the mechanism.

## Public URL candidate

**`sessions.vibejason.com`**

It is shorter than the full product name and names the object the product
controls. `morrow.vibejason.com` would make the operator look like the product
name, which conflicts with the brand boundary above. No DNS or deployment
change is part of this overnight.

## Claims not allowed at launch

- “Run every coding agent in one app.”
- “Supports overnight execution on all six providers.”
- “Morrow understands every conversation.”
- “Your data never leaves your machine.” Model prompts still go to the
  selected provider; only the control plane and indexed metadata are
  local-first.
- “Autonomous,” “fully automatic,” or “set it and forget it.”
- “Verified code” or “verified success.” Morning Review verifies provenance
  and exposes evidence; a person still decides whether the result is correct.
- “Unlimited overnight work” or guaranteed completion by wake time.
- Any public user count, customer logo, testimonial, or performance number
  that does not come from real launch evidence.

## 10,000-user sanity check

Ten thousand installs are plausible only as an outcome of a narrow, repeatable
story—not as a launch forecast. The initial wedge is small but real: developers
who already supervise several local coding agents and feel the human
bottleneck. The launch must first prove three conversion steps:

1. a stranger recognizes the babysitting problem in five seconds;
2. the real product proof makes the bedtime outcome believable;
3. the Mac install and first question work without a trust-breaking detour.

Until those are measured, the honest goal is a high-signal private alpha and a
small group of people who run a second night, not a vanity install number.
